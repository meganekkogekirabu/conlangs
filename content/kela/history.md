History
=======

The Himaq
---------

E asekain 70, Hiullumisetu mowimienta timit make qajiki jumur i jumiuq i
pikung, Esepan i kowierno qisai harama Kela Tichun, mowimiento i masala mi
Kela Parlamentu i parti. In miniuzim kamek miqaji i motin a teroriseta i
hajama, Hiullumisetu eje *Himaq* (Keluchek pekiwa haimaqiu lapaza i tahajuk).
*Himaq* majiqa asekain 80 i mi niuriq pikung, riusun iusu pechi amnisetia ket
ki chiza temokerasiek kowierno pu Kela politikah jenani, a hiu mu harama
Kela Tichun.

In the early 70s, when the Hiullumisetu movement began to gain large amounts
of popular support, the Spanish government attempted to ban the Kela Front,
the party representing the movement in the Canarian parliament. This led to
a series of riots and terrorist attacks referred to as the *Himaq* (a rural
Keluchek pronunciation spelling of *haimaqiu* "struggle") by Hiullumisetu. The
*Himaq* lasted well into the mid 80s, despite some amnesty given by the new
democratic government to Kela political prisoners and the unbanning of the
Kela Front.


Kela christianity
-----------------

According to legend, the Kela were introduced to Christianity in the 5th
century AD by *Haki Iuani* (Saint Johannes), for whom the Kela liturgical rite
is named *Haki Iuani i LLaijiu* (the Liturgy of Saint Johannes). This form of
Christianity is considered heretical by the Catholic Church due to its
adherence to non-Trinitarianism and Arianism. Until the founding of the
republic, it was suppressed by the Spanish crown and today is a minority
religion next to Catholicism, though it has seen some resurgence in recent
years.


Hiullumisemu (Reformism)
------------------------

Kela nasiunalisemu lari laq tiu qaihi pereokupasiun pu Kanari kowierno. E meru
lullait, kuti chaichi saru amet Kela chisi Kanari nasiun separatu ki Esepan. E
Keluchek, kuti nasiunalisetu Kela, ejena Hiullumisetu, i mowimientu ngaiqi
intepentensia Kanari.

Today, Kela nationalism is one of the largest preoccupations for local Canarian
government. In recent days, there are many young Kela people who see the
Canaries as a separate nation from Spain. In Las Palmas, there is a movement of
Kela nationalists who call themselves Reformists and seek to further Canarian
independence.


Orthography reforms
-------------------

In the late 1860s and early 70s, during the liberalisation of the peninsula,
several orthography reforms were floated among Kela liberals, of which very
few gained traction except for the 1869 proposal put forward by Isabela Mioyun
qui Calani, a wealthy liberal from Keluchek (at the time known as Las Palmas).
The 1869 reform did not change much in terms of fundamental spelling besides a
few small differences (particularly, *io* &rarr; *iu* and *y* &rarr; *j*, and a
merger of all of *b*, *gu*, and *hu* into simply *b* for /w/); its most
substantial change was instead a shift away from heavily Spanish-based spellings
for loanwords and toward being spelled phonetically. Some examples include:

- *colonial* &rarr; *coloniah*
- *gobierno* &rarr; *cobiernu*
- *carabelo* &rarr; *carbeh*

It was widely popular and was almost entirely adopted by the end of the 1870s.
It was around this time that more and more Kela were beginning to gain literacy
and the reform helped move along that process by making the orthography easier
to learn. 

In 1956, the Canarian government commissioned another reform which made the
orthography for Kela which is still used today. It removed several of the
idiosyncrasies of Spanish orthography that don't make sense in the context of
Kela:

- *c*, *qu* /k/ &rarr; *k*
- *c*, *qu* /q/ &rarr; *q* (though this had already been adopted in some places)
- *b* /w/ &rarr; *w*
- *-n* /ŋ/ &rarr; *-ng*

The reform also entailed a few unpopular changes like *ll*, *z* &rarr; *lh*,
*lz* and *o* &rarr; *u*, which never saw widespread adoption.


Culture
=======

Paschal traditions
------------------

On *Paseka* (the Kela celebration of Easter):

- Men typically wear *chemang*, a kind of headwear which is made of a pearl
  shell decorated with leaves, traditionally from a hibiscus or a Tenerife
  bugloss. It has been compared to the *dhari* of the Torres Strait Islanders.

- Women wear *huri*, which are necklaces of pearls and flower petals. They are,
  along with the *chemang*, supposed to symbolise the empty tomb (the shells
  and the leaves) and Jesus (the pearls and petals).

- Foods eaten include *chepi* (similar to dumplings), *mari* (fried banana
  dough, usually topped with cherimoya jam or butter), and *chichi* (pastry
  strips filled with jam, usually made of cherimoya or mango).

More recently, with the cultivation of imported mung beans, another pastry was
invented, now eaten on *Paseka* and *Chiza Aseka* (the Kela celebration of the
Hijri New Year), called *paka*, which is similar to baklava sweetened with
mung bean paste instead of syrup or honey.


Akawi
-----

Iusu akawi tailai ki jasai i melain chaki. Te kuti sela sapat telli llaqem i
hapak. Laiwaq newai Roma mequh i *aqua vitae* (pelli i peh), Sekoti i 
*uisge-beatha* (wiseki) weji kin. Iusu chakiuk ma Kela e 10imi senturia
peresentah ki Arapin.

*Akawi* is an alcoholic drink distilled from sweetcorn mash. It is flavoured
with crushed dill seed. The name comes from the Latin *aqua vitae* (water of
life), whence also Scottish *uisge-beatha* (whiskey). Distillation was
introduced to the Kela by the Arabs in the 10th century.


Chija
-----

Chija ngarai Kela, liutiu e ekinoksiu otonah. Ngarai jaikiut nupet husupa i
seremonia teratisiunah a jiucha, ku chepi, ngalza, a riuwum.

*Chija* is a Kela holiday celebrated on the fall equinox. The festivities
typically involve traditional fertility ceremonies and foods like *chepi*
(similar to dumplings), *ngalza* (fried dough), and *riuwum* (a cinnamon-
flavoured hard candy).


Naming conventions
------------------

Typically, Kela names are composed of a Spanish given name (usually biblical,
like *Sara* or *Kariuh*), a Kela given name (*Miujung*, *Keniu*, etc.), and
a matronymic derived from one's mother's Kela name (*ki Miujung*, *ki Kung*,
etc.).
